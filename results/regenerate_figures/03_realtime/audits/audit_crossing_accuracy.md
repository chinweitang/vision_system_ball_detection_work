`summary.txt` reports `median total=105.7mm`, i.e. the same value

### Flights and labelled points

The pooled figure is over the **clean** subset only: flights that are
`symmetric` and not `residual_flagged`. Three of the twenty are asymmetric
(low-confidence) and are excluded, none are residual-flagged. So "20 flights"
describes the labelling effort; **17** describes what the 105.7 mm rests on.

### Axes

`pos_err_total = math.hypot(pos_err_Y, pos_err_Z)` — **in-plane Y and Z
only**, a two-component distance. There is no `pos_err_X` column.

That two-component distance nonetheless **is** the full 3D magnitude here:
both the labelled crossing point and the fitted crossing point are, by
construction, the point where each trajectory intersects the crossing plane,
so both lie on that plane and the depth component is identically zero. It is
in-plane by construction, not by truncation.

### Observation window

**Full arc.** The producer's own header describes the comparison as against
*"Model-C's full-arc-fit crossing-plane state"*. No windowing constant exists
in `src/stereo/label_vs_fit_crossing.py` or in `src/stereo/crossing_plane_classification.py`;
the fit uses every usable point on the track rather than a truncated prefix.
This is the same full-arc reference the convergence figures measure against —
which is why those figures measure convergence, not accuracy, while this one
measures accuracy against independent hand labels.

The three axes are named for the rig, not for the camera frame:
`rms_person_rebounder_mm` is X_world (depth, strong), `rms_width_mm` is
Y_world (**weak**), `rms_vertical_mm` is Z_world (strong). The weak axis is
the widest at 2.40 mm, and the producing script flags it as *somewhat
optimistic* because the board's own tilt mixes the strong axes into it.

All 77 corners come from a single board image, so this is a
precision figure for one pose — not a spread across poses or sessions.
